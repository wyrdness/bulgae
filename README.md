# Bulgae (불개)

## Overview
The Bulgae, meaning "fire dogs" in Korean, are mythological canine creatures from Korean folklore that chase the sun and moon across the sky. According to ancient Korean mythology, when these celestial fire dogs catch either celestial body, they attempt to devour it, causing solar and lunar eclipses. The Bulgae legend represents Korea's ancient astronomical understanding and cultural interpretation of eclipse phenomena, blending shamanic traditions with observational astronomy to explain these dramatic celestial events.

## Description

### Physical Characteristics
- **Appearance**: Massive dog-like creatures composed of fire and flames
- **Size**: Large enough to potentially swallow the sun or moon
- **Color**: Burning red, orange, and yellow flames
- **Eyes**: Blazing with intense fire
- **Fur**: Made entirely of living flames
- **Teeth**: Sharp, capable of biting celestial bodies
- **Behavior**: Constantly chasing the sun and moon
- **Number**: Typically depicted as a pair (one for sun, one for moon)

### Supernatural Abilities
- **Celestial Movement**: Can run through the sky at incredible speeds
- **Fire Form**: Body composed of supernatural flames
- **Immortality**: Cannot be killed by conventional means
- **Persistence**: Never tire in their eternal chase
- **Celestial Touch**: Ability to grasp sun and moon
- **Eclipse Creation**: Temporarily devour celestial bodies

## Etymology and Linguistic Origins

### Name Meaning
The word "Bulgae" (불개) breaks down as:
- **Bul (불)**: Fire in Korean
- **Gae (개)**: Dog in Korean
- **Combined Meaning**: Fire dog or flame hound

### Regional Names
Different Korean regions use variations:
- **Bulgae**: Most common modern term
- **Bulguri**: Older dialectical variation
- **Cheongung-gae**: Heavenly dog (formal term)
- **Sik-gae**: Eclipse dog (descriptive term)

## Korean Astronomical Mythology

### Eclipse Explanation
Ancient Koreans interpreted eclipses through the Bulgae legend:

**Solar Eclipses**:
- The Bulgae catches and bites the sun
- The sun's light dims as it's partially devoured
- Human intervention through noise helps drive the dog away
- The sun gradually reappears as it escapes

**Lunar Eclipses**:
- Another Bulgae pursues and catches the moon
- The moon turns red or dark during the attack
- Similar rescue rituals performed
- Moon recovers as the dog releases it

### Cultural Response to Eclipses
Traditional Korean communities responded to eclipses with specific rituals:
- **Noise Making**: Banging pots, drums, and gongs to scare the Bulgae
- **Shouting**: Collective yelling to drive away the fire dogs
- **Arrow Shooting**: Symbolic arrows shot toward the eclipsed body
- **Prayers**: Appeals to deities to save the sun or moon
- **Offerings**: Food and incense to celestial protectors

## Comparison with Other Cultures

### Chinese Tiangou (天狗)
The Chinese celestial dog:
- **Similarities**: Also causes eclipses by devouring celestial bodies
- **Differences**: Often associated with meteors and comets too
- **Influence**: Likely influenced Korean Bulgae concept
- **Variations**: Different cultural interpretations

### Norse Sköll and Hati
Norse wolves that chase sun and moon:
- **Similarities**: Perpetual pursuit of celestial bodies
- **Differences**: More apocalyptic (Ragnarök connection)
- **Cultural Function**: Similar explanatory role
- **Mythological Context**: Different cosmological framework

### Hindu Rahu and Ketu
Hindu demons that cause eclipses:
- **Similarities**: Attack sun and moon periodically
- **Differences**: Severed head of demon, more complex story
- **Ritual Response**: Similar protective ceremonies
- **Astronomical**: More sophisticated mathematical understanding

## Eclipse Rituals and Practices

### Traditional Community Response
When eclipses occurred:

**Immediate Actions**:
1. Ring bells and gongs continuously
2. Beat drums and create loud noise
3. Shoot arrows toward sky
4. Shout collective warnings
5. Children cry and make noise

**Protective Measures**:
1. Cover wells and water sources (believed contaminated)
2. Bring children indoors
3. Light fires and torches
4. Burn incense and offerings
5. Pray at temples and shrines

**Post-Eclipse**:
1. Purification rituals
2. Thanksgiving ceremonies
3. Community feasts
4. Shamanic cleansing
5. Return to normal activities
